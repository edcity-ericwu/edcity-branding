EdCity · Brand direction test

Parent Update, Three Ways

One scenario, one message, built to full structural divergence across the three candidate directions — not a palette swap. If these read as one screen in three colours, the direction work is not carrying its weight. Squint, or hit the grayscale test, and check whether you can still tell them apart.

Scenario
A parent (陳太) opens EdCity. Her daughter 陳可欣, Primary 4, has two things worth knowing this week: reading fluency has moved up a level, and she has been leading a class recycling project.
Fixed
Same parent, same child, same two facts, same moment in the app — on all three boards.
Varies
What is on screen · what the parent does · how the system shows itself · how progress is depicted · the voice of the copy.
Removes colour from the three mocks. Distinct information design survives it; a re-skinned template does not.

A dated note, three sentences, one highlighted talking point, a reply link.

Read; reply to the teacher — a conversation, not a confirmation.
System presence
Behind the teacher’s voice — it helps draft, it does not speak.

This is: human, respectful, encouraging. This is not: childish, or soft on rigour. Risk in the room: “where is the authority and technical credibility?”

What to look at

Down each column: element count, number of things to do, and whether the system speaks. 01 gives one instruction; 02 shows its reasoning and branches out; 03 stays a message from a person.

The lever that splits the room

System presence. Silent line vs. named rationale vs. teacher’s voice maps onto a real unresolved question — how much should EdCity show its workings to a parent?

Greenlight the same treatment for the other two shared moments — teacher AI support and student next-step — then assemble the full boards around them.
