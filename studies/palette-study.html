A warm, grounded palette built from the moodboard's sunrise-over-mountains photo — a calm landscape opening up at first light, spacious and easy to move through. A clear, legible blue carries structure and wayfinding, drawn straight from the photo's own mountain-shadow tone; tan and rose-beige bring warmth and material close to hand; apricot glow and dusty coral mark where an opportunity opens, one closer and more frequent, one rare and stronger. How to read it

A landscape at first light: a steady navy field, warm materials close to hand, and two points of colour where something is beginning — one that appears often, one that appears rarely and means more when it does. The layout mirrors how the colours divide a screen — it isn’t the identity itself.

The field

Slate navy holds most of any screen — navigation, framing, the steady background a teacher returns to. Deep slate navy takes text and edges. Together they keep the interface quiet and readable at a glance, and the hue stays clearly blue — the mountain-shadow tone in the photo, not a flattened grey.

The earth tones

Tan and rose beige do the warm work: photography edges, card surfaces, subject or department colour-coding. Warm neutrals against navy, matching the moodboard's swatch. They give the system a human, material feel without pulling focus from the task.

The two sparks

Apricot glow is the closer, more frequent signal — a surfaced opportunity, a next step worth taking. Pulled straight from the moodboard's sunrise photo: the warm band low on the horizon. Dusty coral sits behind it as the rarer, stronger mark — reserved for the single most important call in a view, so it never competes with apricot for attention. Use either as a mark, a fill, an underline, or large display, paired with dark ink; never both at full strength in the same moment.

The clear note

Clear sky is the one saturated, cool colour in the family — reserved for links, focus rings, and active/selected states. It reads as the moment the haze lifts, not as a brand colour competing with the sparks. Small footprint, always functional, never decorative.

What it’s for

Screens a teacher lives in every day: calm, structured, warm enough to trust. Moments of guidance and encouragement. Print and editorial material where the earth tones can lead.

Spark & Light

Colour and light do the expressive work. The field stays flat and matte; energy comes from the spark and from gradients built out of the palette. Each screen uses at most one gradient surface, on a real moment.

Bridge tones — gradients only

Sun Haze #F2C089 Sun Disc #FDEBC7 Mist Rose #E8B9AE

Sun haze, sun disc, and mist rose exist to make the gradients glow — lifted directly from the sunrise photo's horizon and fog bands. They belong inside a blend, not as flat colour.

Use a gradient for

  • The moment an opportunity is surfaced
  • Onboarding and first run
  • Milestone and progress moments
  • Marketing and campaign heroes
  • Loading and transition states
  • Light overlays on photography

Never

  • Navigation, headers, sidebars, cards
  • Buttons or interactive controls
  • Behind body text
  • More than one gradient surface in a view
  • The daily screens — dashboards, lists, forms
  • First light on a light ground — it’s made for dark surfaces

Motion: first light reveals slowly on load, then holds. Movement stays gentle and peripheral, and it honours reduced-motion.
