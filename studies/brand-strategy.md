## Brand Personality

> EdCity brand is a calm, capable guide: it makes complexity understandable, helps people make progress with confidence, and earns trust through evidence — not noise. The personality should feel human-first and expertly governed, with AI working quietly in the background.

Three reference figures, one per personality facet:

| Facet | Reference | What it means for EdCity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **For clarity** — how we make things clear | Zhou Youguang (周有光), 1906–2017 — accessible systems, clarity, standards, inclusive access | Thoughtful systems make access feel natural. EdCity uses clear standards, structure, and quiet intelligence to help people find, understand, and act on what matters. |
| **For form** — how we create structure | Tadao Ando — structure, restraint, light, hierarchy, deliberate pathways | Calm structure creates confidence. Use deliberate space, hierarchy, and restraint to turn a complex environment into a clear path forward. |
| **For behaviour** — how we assist | James Turrell's Skyspace — quiet assistance, focus, perception, human agency | Intelligence that creates clarity, not spectacle. EdCity should frame what matters, helping people notice, understand, and choose without taking over. |

**EdCity is a calm, capable guide.** It combines accessible expertise, purposeful structure, and quiet intelligence so people can make progress with confidence. The personality should feel human-first, evidence-led, and well-governed, with AI working quietly in the background.

### How it looks
- **Structured, not sterile**: clear hierarchy, generous whitespace, modular layouts, progressive disclosure, and visible pathways through complex information.
- **Credible warmth**: refined editorial typography paired with accessible UI; use restrained colour with purposeful highlights rather than high-alert saturation.
- **Intelligent assistance**: recommendations, summaries, and next-best actions should be embedded in context — not presented as flashy AI spectacle.
- **Inclusive realism**: use varied ages, roles, abilities, and everyday settings; avoid generic "future-tech" imagery.
- **Calm progress cues**: show status, milestones, and reassurance without countdowns, red urgency states, or performance theatre.

### How it talks
- **Plain and precise**: "Here's what matters," "Next, you can...," "Based on the available evidence..."
- **Supportive, never patronising**: explain choices and preserve user agency: "You're in control," not "We know what's best."
- **Transparent and accountable**: name sources, data recency, confidence, ownership, and limitations.
- **Calm under pressure**: frame exceptions as manageable actions, e.g. "This needs attention by Friday" rather than "Urgent: act now."
- **Respectful of context**: adapt language, reading level, and guidance to role, confidence, age, and situation.

### How it behaves
- **Organises before asking**: turns messy inputs into clear priorities, summaries, and actionable next steps.
- **Assists without taking over**: recommends and automates reversible tasks; requests confirmation for consequential actions.
- **Explains its reasoning**: shows why a recommendation appears and lets users inspect, edit, or decline it.
- **Builds confidence gradually**: offers guided paths for newcomers and fast routes for experienced users.
- **Protects trust by default**: uses governance, permissions, auditability, and clear data controls as visible product behaviours — not back-office promises.

### Personality boundaries

| Be | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Assured, evidence-led, and composed | Corporate, bureaucratic, or cold |
| Helpful and empowering | Overbearing, corrective, or paternalistic |
| Smart but understated | "Magic AI" jargon, or anthropomorphic hype |
| Calm and progress-oriented | Gamified pressure, alarmism, or hustle language |
| Inclusive and situationally aware | One-size-fits-all flows or token diversity |

## Visual Anchors

**Paths, Knowledge, Connection.**
> A calm, trusted wayfinding layer for Hong Kong education.

EdCity does not ask people to learn the system. It helps them find their way through it. EdCity is a trusted wayfinding layer for education: it connects people and services, organises verified knowledge, and makes the next step clear.

- **Primary anchor**: Wayfinding
- **Supporting anchors**: Library, bridge, connected neighbourhood

| Area | Translation |
| --- | --- |
| Navigation | Make the next relevant route prominent; use contextual breadcrumbs and cross-links rather than deep menus. |
| Information architecture | Organise by user intent and life/school moments, not internal departments or service silos. |
| Colour | Use a stable neutral base; assign restrained, accessible route colours by domain or task, rather than one loud brand colour everywhere. |
| Components | Treat cards and modules as "destinations": clear title, purpose, eligibility, source, status, and next action. |
| AI patterns | Position AI as a guide that clarifies choices, connects services, and explains recommendations — not a conversational mascot. |
| Content design | Use signpost language: "Start here," "For your role," "What happens next," "You can return to this later." |
| Motion | Use motion to orient — revealing relationships, showing where something moved, and confirming progress — not to create spectacle. |

### Supporting anchor — wayfinding
Use the logic of wayfinding — not literal MTR branding or transport visuals. Look for clear routes, consistent label systems, recognised destinations, predictable landmarks, and visible progress. (Reference searches: urban wayfinding map graphic design, museum wayfinding signage typography, public library wayfinding interior.)

### Supporting anchor — library catalogue
A library is a better knowledge reference than a "digital learning portal." It naturally signals curated trust, diverse needs, self-directed discovery, and quiet competence. Maps to product:
- The cataloguing layer = structured data, governance, content standards, evidence, and metadata.
- The reading room = calm focus and human progress.
- The librarian = guidance without control.
- The collection = diverse EdTech, support services, and learning resources.

A key distinction: do not visually recreate an old-fashioned, solemn library. Use the principle of a modern public library — transparent, navigable, useful, inclusive, and community-serving. (Reference searches: library catalogue index card system, Helsinki Oodi library interior, modern library signage design, public library community learning space.)

### Supporting anchor — bridge / connections across a city
A bridge gives the "super-connector" idea a human, physical form. It should be a pedestrian bridge, footpath, or connected public space — not corporate infrastructure or stock-image handshake. It can communicate:
- Education is a shared ecosystem rather than a collection of separate apps.
- Progress involves crossing between stages: home ↔ school, primary ↔ secondary, learning ↔ wellbeing, exploration ↔ action.
- The brand creates a safe, clear transition — not a platform that claims to own every destination.

(Reference searches: community bridge architecture public space, connected neighbourhood aerial pedestrian pathways.)

These three are candidates for the next content/device pass, not something to build silently — flagging for your call before touching the shared skeleton.
