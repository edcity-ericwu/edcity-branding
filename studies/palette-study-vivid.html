How to read it

A bright sky-blue field on cool near-white, with pale surfaces close to hand and two vivid points that appear only where something happens. The layout mirrors how the colours divide a screen — it isn’t the identity itself.

The backbone

Main Blue carries structure, navigation, actions, and links. Lighter and airier than the other options, so the interface reads open rather than institutional. It is not dark enough for body text on white — Ink does that; Main Blue stays on framing, fills, and large type.

The field

Near-white ground plus three pale tints — cyan, peach, pink — for quiet surfaces, hover, and subject colour-coding. Most of every screen stays this light.

The two points

Signal Pink marks an opportunity surfacing. Signal Green marks something confirmed or complete. Each is small and rare; they do not appear together in one view unless one of them is a state changing to the other. Neither is body text.

Where this sits

Younger and higher-energy than the other options, and less grounded — more student- and family-facing. Strongest for onboarding, campaigns, and the moments meant to feel alive.

What it’s for

Front-of-house: marketing, onboarding, student and parent views. The daily teacher UI would pull the points right back and lean on blue, paper, and ink.

Two Points & Light

Two vivid points and a set of light gradients carry the energy. Pink for an opportunity opening, green for a step completed. One point per moment; one gradient surface per screen.

Bridge tones — gradients only

Lavender #B9A8F2 Mint #9DF2C4

Lavender carries blue into pink, mint carries paper into green. They live inside a blend, not as flat colour.

Use a point / gradient for

  • The moment an opportunity surfaces (pink)
  • A step completed or confirmed (green)
  • Onboarding and first run
  • Marketing and campaign heroes
  • Loading and transition states
  • Ambient background behind an empty screen

Never

  • Both points in one view, unless one is changing into the other
  • A point as body text or a large fill
  • Navigation, headers, sidebars, cards
  • More than one gradient surface in a view
  • The spectrum gradient behind product content
  • The daily teacher screens — dial the points right back

Motion: gradients drift slowly or reveal on load. A point may pulse once when it appears — never continuously — and it honours reduced-motion.
